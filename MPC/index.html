MPC Live Engine: How a dynamic QP is built and solved every cycle

A fired heater. MV = fuel valve (%). CV1 = coil outlet temperature (track the setpoint). CV2 = tube skin temperature (high limit). Skin temp reacts faster and with a higher gain than outlet temp, so pushing hard for outlet temp can overshoot the skin limit on the way there. That's exactly what the dynamic layer checks. Nobody types an equation here: the engine builds H, g, C, b from step-response models and tuning, then solves a QP (ADMM, OSQP-style) live in your browser.

Dynamic matrix A (CV1): the "equation"

Rows = future minutes k+1..k+P. Columns = future moves Δu0..ΔuM-1. Each column is the step-test curve shifted down one minute. Dead time shows as the dark top-left triangle. Prediction = y_free + A·Δu: just multiplication, which is why it runs in milliseconds.

Things to try

1. Dynamic vs steady-state constraint: untick Enforce CV2 limit and hit Reset. The SS target is still legal at 597.5 °C, but the aggressive path overshoots the skin limit to ~615 °C. Tick it again and the QP bends the MV path so skin temp rides the limit on the way up. The SS optimiser can't see this; only the dynamic QP checks every future minute.

2. Tube fouling: the SS layer sees the skin limit bind and lowers the CV1 target below SP (SP not reachable). That's the constraint priority logic, done by the LP, not by the operator.

3. Move suppression: slide WΔu up and watch the purple plan flatten, and the move-cost bar grow while tracking cost rises. That's the trade-off the QP is balancing.

4. Model mismatch 1.4×: predictions are now wrong. The bias update corrects it cycle by cycle, but you'll see small transient limit violations. That's why real APC keeps a safety margin and why step-testing quality matters.

5. M = 1: the controller can plan only one move, so it becomes timid and slow. Longer M gives the QP more freedom to shape the path.
